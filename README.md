# CHEM1043 · General Chemistry II

An exam-first study collection for the HKU Moodle section **1A, 2026–27**. Start with the [past-paper workflow guide](past-papers/README.md), use its representative questions to diagnose gaps, and consult the [lecture-material index](lectures/README.md) only for the concepts needed to unblock a workflow.

## Course in brief

The current course materials cover six connected areas: gas laws and kinetic theory; molecular orbitals and band theory; solutions and colligative properties; solubility, precipitation and complex-ion equilibria; entropy and Gibbs energy; and electrochemistry. These are the topics listed in the [CHEM1043 Moodle course and its syllabus](https://moodle.hku.hk/course/view.php?id=139035).

- **Gases:** move between measurements, moles and composition, then recognize when ideal-gas assumptions fail.
- **Molecular orbitals:** turn electron counts and orbital ordering into bond order, magnetism and bonding explanations.
- **Solutions:** choose the correct concentration measure and connect particle concentration or partial pressure to a measurable property.
- **Solubility and complexes:** combine stoichiometry, mass balance and equilibrium constants; distinguish dissolved total metal from free ions.
- **Thermodynamics:** connect reaction data, entropy, Gibbs energy, equilibrium and direction under actual conditions.
- **Electrochemistry:** balance the reaction before linking voltage, Gibbs energy, equilibrium and concentration.

This makes the historical topic structure useful for organizing practice, not for forecasting an exact paper. Henry's law, Raoult's law, MO analysis, particle-based solution properties, precipitation boundaries and Gibbs/equilibrium reasoning recur across the retrieved set; the [workflow guide](past-papers/README.md#workflow-frequency) separates row frequency from distinct-paper counts.

**Predictability is limited:** all four accessible papers are open-book exams from 2020–2022, while all 16 other ExamBase records were checked individually and marked restricted access. A catalogue listing is not evidence of its questions, and the old open-book rules do not establish the current exam's permitted materials. Newer papers, different teachers and changed assessment conditions could change both format and emphasis.

## Study route and repository map

1. Open the [workflow guide](past-papers/README.md) and attempt a priority row before opening slides. Priority means representative within its workflow, not solved or predicted.
2. Review the minimum prerequisites and method for the blocked step, retry independently, then attempt a different row in the same group.
3. Mix workflows under timed conditions and include every current syllabus topic; do not omit a topic just because it is absent from this small historical sample.
